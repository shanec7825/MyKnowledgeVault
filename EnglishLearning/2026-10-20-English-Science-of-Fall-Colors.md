# 2026-10-20 · Science of Fall Colors

## Listening · 6 min

**First pass (3 min):** Listen without reading. Reconstruct **activity people enjoy → changing weather → examples → biological mechanism → potential consequence**. Do not stop for place names.

**Locate the break (2 min):** Pick exactly one uncertain relationship: **before** the best colors appear, **instead of** a gradual change, **can cause** early leaf drop, or the contrast between **shorter days** and **very dry summers**. Open only the relevant transcript lines; replay 15–25 seconds; name the subject, cause, and effect.

**Retrieve (1 min):** Hide the transcript and explain: “Shorter days help trigger a normal seasonal change, while unusual heat or drought can affect its timing and quality.” Treat this as a distinction to reconstruct, not as a script to memorize.

## Reading · 9 min

**Read the NPS article** from “Why Do Leaves Change Color in Autumn?” through the weather factors and examples. The goal is its **explanatory structure**, not translating every word.

**1. Label functions (3 min):** Mark **SEASONAL CONTEXT → NORMAL MECHANISM → PIGMENT EXAMPLES → MODULATING CONDITIONS → VISIBLE OUTCOME**. For each label, locate one supporting sentence or detail.

**2. Rebuild (2 min):**

```text
SHORTER DAYS / seasonal signals
            ↓
less ______ produced
            ↓
green fades → existing ______ pigments become visible
            ↘
sunlight + moisture + temperature influence ______ pigments/intensity
            ↓
colors differ by species and local ______
```

**3. Sentence-to-model repair (2 min):** In the sentence about underlying pigments “taking over,” what exactly changes: new yellow pigments suddenly appear, or green pigment becomes less dominant? For red and purple, what additional pigment may be present? Explain why “cooler temperature” is not the whole explanation.

**4. Evidence and boundary (2 min):** Compare the NPS biological mechanism with the VOA 2021 examples. Does a weather-related delay in one region prove a long-term global trend? Which source supplies **mechanism**, and which supplies **reported cases and expert interpretation**? Summarize in **one 25-word English sentence** from memory.

**Local → global check:** Parse **“As chlorophyll production ends ... underlying pigments ... take over.”** Identify the time relation (*as*), the process, and the result. Then place it inside the article's full explanation.

## Active Expressions · 4 min

### [[words/be-shaped-by|be shaped by]]

**Retrieve from Chinese:** “秋叶颜色受到树种和天气的共同影响。” → Fall colors ___ ___ ___ tree species and weather.

**Pattern:** **X is shaped by Y** = Y influences X (not necessarily its only cause). *Fall colors are shaped by sunlight, moisture, and the kinds of trees present.*

**Transfer:** Describe how a student's learning experience is shaped by feedback and available time.

### [[words/not-necessarily|not necessarily]]

**Retrieve:** “较热的秋天不一定意味着所有树木都会更晚变色。” → A warmer autumn does ___ ___ mean every tree changes color later.

**Scope:** **not necessarily** weakens a conclusion; it does not mean **never**. Compare “This can affect color” with “This always changes color.”

**Transfer:** Make a careful claim about AI-generated answers or team performance.

### Review · [[words/up-to-a-point|up to a point]]

**Retrieve:** “更长时间的阳光在一定程度上有帮助，但水分也重要。” → More sunlight may help ___ ___ ___, but moisture also matters.

**Pronunciation (30 sec):** stress **SHAPED**, **NEC** in *necessarily*, and **POINT**. Read a cause → qualification sentence aloud, then substitute **study habits** for **leaf colors**.

## Speaking · 4 min

**Topic: Explaining autumn colors without overstating the evidence.** Answer four progressively harder questions (about 45–60 seconds in total, then retry one weak answer):

1. What changes in a leaf as the days grow shorter?
2. Why might two trees show different autumn colors?
3. How do you distinguish the **seasonal mechanism** from the **weather conditions** affecting its appearance?
4. A friend says, “One late autumn proves climate change.” How would you respond fairly, and what further evidence would you seek?
5. Could the same **mechanism vs. influencing factors** distinction help explain why two students learn differently?

Use **be shaped by** and **not necessarily** without looking. Note the first word or logical link you could not retrieve: ___
