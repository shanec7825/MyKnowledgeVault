# 2026-10-23 · AI Privacy Choices

**Big question:** How can an AI assistant remain useful while collecting less personal information?

**30 minutes:** Listening 6 · Reading 9 · Active Expressions 4 · Speaking 4 · Writing 5 · Retrieval/Reflection 2.

**Method:** complete → find an exact breakdown → repair → transfer to a new situation → retrieve. Train sentence relations → whole-text model, and recognition → retrieval.

## Listening · 6 min

**Complete (3 min):** Listen to 0:00–2:45 without transcript. Give a two-sentence gist: **privacy problem → possible user choices → important qualification**. Ignore interface details.

**Break and repair (2 min):** Find one precise 15–25-second misunderstanding. Does **some tools** mean **all tools**? Does opting out of model training imply all conversation data is immediately deleted? Replay only that span, then check the matching transcript paragraph.

**Transfer (1 min):** Hide the text and explain the difference between **changing future training use** and **deleting past conversations**, without promising either offers perfect privacy.

## Reading · 9 min

**Material:** ICO's official guidance linked above. Read **At a glance**, **What is the data minimisation principle?**, **How do we decide?**, and skim the too-much / inadequate examples.

1. **Structure (3 min):** Label five parts: **purpose → three tests → reason → too-much example → not-enough counterexample**. Give a 3–6-word clue for each.
2. **Local sentence (2 min):** Parse **adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary**. Which word means enough, which means related to the purpose, and which excludes unnecessary details?
3. **Transfer (2 min):** Compare a university notice bot and a personalised study assistant. Which information might each require? Why is **minimum** not **zero**?
4. **Retrieval (2 min):** Close the page and rebuild:

```text

       → too much: ______
       → too little: ______

```

Summarise the whole argument in one 25–35-word English sentence.

<details><summary>Repair hint — open only after trying</summary>

Adequate = enough for the purpose; relevant = connected to the purpose; limited = no unnecessary extras. The text warns about both collecting too much and collecting too little.
