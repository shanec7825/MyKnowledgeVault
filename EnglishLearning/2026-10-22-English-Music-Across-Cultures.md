# 2026-10-22 · Music Across Cultures

**Big question:** How can music preserve a local identity while sharing features with music elsewhere?

**30 minutes:** Listening **6** · Reading **9** · Active Expressions **4** · Speaking **4** · Writing **5** · Retrieval/Reflection **2**.

**Method:** complete → locate one exact comprehension break → repair that sentence → transfer to a new situation → retrieve and reflect. The target is **sentence relation → whole-text model** and **recognition → independent retrieval**.

**Source boundary:** VOA describes Appalachian *old-time* music as a particular cultural tradition, shaped by several historical influences. Smithsonian reports a **different 2024 study** comparing song and speech across 55 languages. These are not the same study. The observed similarities do **not** prove that every culture's music is identical or establish why humans evolved to sing.

## Listening · 6 min

**Complete (3 min):** Listen to the specified VOA excerpt **without transcript**. Ignore instrument names on the first pass. Reconstruct **migration → traditions brought along → cultural mixing → new regional style → community function**. Say your gist in two English sentences.

**Find and repair (2 min):** Identify one *exact* break, for example: Does **“continued the ... traditions”** mean the music stayed unchanged? How does **“over time”** connect European and African influences to a regional style? Replay a 15–25-second span and check **only** the corresponding transcript paragraph. State what changed in your interpretation.

**Retrieve (1 min):** Hide the text and explain the difference between **preserving an influence** and **creating something new through mixing**. Use a music or food example of your own.

**Actual evidence, to fill only after listening:** timestamp / words ___ · first interpretation ___ · corrected relationship ___.

## Reading · 9 min

**Material:** Smithsonian's 2024 report above; read the opening, method, three findings, proposed interpretation, and sample limitations. Aim for the *argument*, not translation.

1. **Structure (3 min):** Assign these functions to the article's parts: **QUESTION → CROSS-CULTURAL METHOD → MEASURED RESULTS → POSSIBLE EXPLANATION → LIMITATION**. Write a short evidence clue for each, using your own words.
2. **Local sentence → global model (2 min):** Parse the statement that singing **tends to** be slower than speech. Does it claim **every** song is slower? Identify the compared activities, the direction of the comparison, and the hedge. Place this result in the *measured results* part of the argument.
3. **Evidence boundary (2 min):** The researchers recorded traditional songs, spoken lyrics, and instrumental versions. Which observations are **acoustic measurements**? Which idea—music may support social bonding—is an **interpretive hypothesis**? Why do small per-language samples matter?
4. **Rebuild (2 min):** Close the article. Complete the model from memory:

```text
WHY DO PEOPLE SING? (question)
        ↓
55 languages: songs + spoken lyrics + ______ (comparison)
        ↓
three tendencies: slower ______ / steadier ______ / higher ______
        ↓
possible function: ______ (hypothesis, not proof)
        ↓
limits: few ______ per language; performer-selection effects
```

Then compress the report into **one 25-word sentence**, explicitly distinguishing a finding from an explanation.

<details><summary>Repair hint — only after attempting</summary>

“Tends to” describes a tendency across observations, not a rule for every song. A study of sound patterns can identify regularities without proving the evolutionary purpose of singing. The researchers' sample was broad across languages but shallow within many languages.

</details>

## Active Expressions · 4 min

### [[words/pass-down|pass down]]

**Meaning → English:** “把音乐传统传给下一代” → ___ ___ a musical tradition to the next generation.

**Use:** Families can **pass down** songs, stories, or skills **from one generation to another**. It implies transmission, not perfect preservation. **Transfer:** Describe how a programming club passes down practical debugging habits.

### [[words/bring-together|bring ... together]]

**Meaning → English:** “音乐把不同年龄的人联系在一起” → Music can ___ people of different ages ___.

**Use:** A shared activity can **bring people together**, even when they have different backgrounds. **Transfer:** Use it to describe a university group project, not a concert.

### Review · [[words/be-shaped-by|be shaped by]]

**Retrieve:** “音乐既受到当地文化影响，也受到人的共同心理特征影响。” → Musical practices may ___ ___ ___ local culture **and** shared human tendencies.

**Precision + pronunciation (30 sec):** Stress **PASS DOWN**, **TOGETHER** (second syllable), **SHAPED**. In speech, link *pass‿down*. Say one sentence with **both ... and ...**, then reconstruct it without looking. “Be shaped by” means **influenced by**, not **entirely determined by**.

## Speaking · 4 min

**Topic: What makes music local—and shared?** Answer progressively; target 20–35 seconds per question, then retry the first answer that stalled.

1. What kind of music is closely associated with a place or community you know?
2. How might a family or school **pass down** that music?
3. How can making music **bring people together** even when they have different tastes?
4. Compare the **historical story** in the VOA article with the **measured cross-language patterns** in Smithsonian. What does each source actually show?
5. If someone claims “music is exactly the same everywhere,” how would you respond using **tends to** and one limitation?

## Writing · 5 min

**Write 80–120 original English words:** **Should universities offer optional music-sharing events where students introduce songs from their own backgrounds?** State a position, propose one specific activity, explain a possible cultural or social benefit, and acknowledge a limitation (such as personal taste or the risk of oversimplifying traditions). Use **pass down**, **bring ... together**, or **be shaped by** accurately. Do **not** copy a model essay.
