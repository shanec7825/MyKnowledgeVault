## Listening · 6 min

**Material:** VOA's 3:49 report introduces a 2015 root exhibition and moves from visible roots to hidden biological functions.

1. **Complete (4 min):** Listen once with transcript hidden. Retrieve a **four-part map**: invisible roots → exhibition → water/nutrient uptake → roots and bacteria. Write only 4 labels.
2. **Find a break (1 min):** Replay **one 15–25-second span**. Was the breakdown a noun (*nodules*), the explanation of nitrogen, or a pronoun's referent? Check only that line in the transcript.
3. **Transfer (1 min):** Without notes, explain why the **roots** and the **bacteria in certain root nodules** do different jobs. Do not say that every plant root fixes atmospheric nitrogen.

> **Real listening evidence (complete after listening):** timestamp ___; first interpretation ___; exact repair ___; whole-report model ___; new-context sentence ___.

<details><summary>After attempting: local-to-global clue</summary>

The report describes roots absorbing water and nutrients. In its soybean example, microorganisms in root nodules convert nitrogen into a usable form. These are distinct functions. The 2015 exhibit is an illustration, not a controlled experiment.

</details>

## Reading · 9 min

**Read the two official texts linked above; keep the originals in their own pages.** Do not copy them into your note.

1. **Structure (3 min):** Label the Botanic Garden's sections as **hidden importance → ways to make roots visible → soil as a living system**. Then label the USDA explainer **problem (nitrogen supply) → exception (microorganisms) → mechanism (root nodules)**. Which text explains *how*, and which explains *why people should notice*?
2. **Sentence-level repair (2 min):** In USDA, locate the sentence containing **78%**. Does that number describe nitrogen in the **air**, nitrogen usable by **all plants**, or nitrogen already in **soil**? Identify the scope of *most* and the exception. One changed referent changes the entire argument.
3. **Mechanism check (2 min):** Rebuild this sequence **without looking**: atmospheric nitrogen → rhizobia inside legume root nodules → usable nitrogen compound → plant growth. Explain what the plant supplies in return. Distinguish the nitrogen-fixing partnership from roots' ordinary uptake of soil water and minerals.
4. **Retrieval (2 min):** Close both sources. Give a 40–55-word account with **importance → mechanism → boundary → implication**, using no more than one specialist term.

<details><summary>Check after retrieval, not before</summary>

The 78% refers to atmospheric nitrogen gas, not plant-available soil nitrogen. Most organisms cannot use nitrogen gas directly; certain microorganisms can transform it. USDA describes a particular symbiosis involving legumes and rhizobia, not a universal property of all roots.

</details>

## Active Expressions · 4 min

### [[words/play-a-role-in|play a role in ...]] — contribute to a process

- **Meaning → English:** “根系在减少土壤侵蚀方面发挥作用。” → Roots ___ ___ ___ ___ reducing soil erosion.
- **Pattern:** *X plays a role in + noun / -ing*. Say **ROLE** clearly; don't say *play a role to reduce*.
- **Transfer:** Explain one factor that plays a role in effective group projects.

### [[words/in-turn|in turn]] — a consequence or reciprocal step

- **Meaning → English:** “细菌提供可用氮，植物反过来提供糖。” → The bacteria provide usable nitrogen; the plant, ___ ___, supplies sugars.
- **Pattern:** *X happens; this, in turn, affects Y.* Distinguish a causal chain from merely saying *also*.
- **Transfer:** Explain a chain in learning: feedback → better revision → improved clarity.

### Review [[words/rely-on|rely on ...]] — depend on

- **Recall from Chinese:** “植物依靠根部吸收水分。” → Plants ___ ___ roots to absorb water.
- **Transfer:** Say what a software project relies on besides code.

**No-peeking test:** Combine *play a role in* + *in turn* in **one new sentence** about a sports team or programming. Then retrieve *rely on* without a cue.

## Speaking · 4 min

**Theme:** The invisible systems we depend on. Answer aloud without a script.

1. What can plant roots do besides hold a plant in place?
2. Why does the USDA text discuss nitrogen even though the air contains so much of it?
3. How do the roles of roots and root-associated bacteria differ?
4. What invisible system in a university or a computer program plays a similar supporting role? Explain the analogy's **limit**.
