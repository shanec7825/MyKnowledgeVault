# 2026-10-17 · Shade and City Heat

![Topic illustration](assets/art/2026-10-17-Shade-and-City-Heat.svg)

**Big question:** Why do some city neighborhoods stay hotter than others, and how should communities respond?

**30 minutes:** Listening 6 · Reading 9 · Active Expressions 4 · Speaking 4 · Writing 5 · Retrieval/Reflection 2.

## Reading · 9 min

Read the EPA article: first the cooling mechanism, then co-benefits and unequal access. Label the structure **mechanism → evidence → benefits → equity → scope**.

**Visual retrieval:**

```text
Unequal urban greenery → different shade exposure
Trees: shade + evapotranspiration → local cooling
Research averages ≠ guaranteed outcome on every street
```

The EPA discussion cites a review of 308 studies reporting urban forests approximately 1.6°C cooler on average than non-green urban areas. Explain why that **average** is not a guarantee for every campus.

**Answer without looking:** What are the two cooling mechanisms? What changes when shade is unevenly available? What evidence would you need for your own campus?

## Active Expressions · 4 min

- **be unevenly distributed** — not spread equally. From Chinese, retrieve: “树荫在不同街区分布不均。” Transfer to unequal access to quiet study areas.
- **take ... into account** — consider a factor when deciding. Retrieve: “规划时考虑维护成本和居民需求。” Transfer to AI model latency and privacy.
- **Review: weigh X against Y** — balance benefits against costs. Retrieve: “权衡绿化收益与维护成本。”

**Pronunciation:** UNevenly disTRIButed / across NEIGHborhoods · TAKE residents' NEEDS / into acCOUNT.

## Speaking · 4 min

1. Which campus areas probably feel hottest, and why?
2. How are shade and evapotranspiration different?
3. What does a research average *not* prove?
4. Who might be affected by uneven shade access?
5. How would you weigh planting trees against artificial shade if resources were limited?

Speak for 45–60 seconds. Include a new expression, a comparison, and a limitation.

## Writing · 5 min

**80–120 words.** Should universities prioritize planting trees or building artificial shade structures to make outdoor study areas more comfortable? Give a position, a physical mechanism, a campus example, a trade-off, and one measurement that could change your mind.
