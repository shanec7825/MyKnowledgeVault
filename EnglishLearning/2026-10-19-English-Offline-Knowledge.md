# 2026-10-19 · Offline Knowledge

![Topic illustration](assets/art/2026-10-19-Offline-Knowledge.svg)

> Visual companion: illustrated navigation is available in the linked HTML page.

**Core question:** Can students keep learning when the internet is unreliable?

**30 minutes:** Listening 6 · Reading 9 · Active Expressions 4 · Speaking 4 · Writing 5 · Retrieval/Reflection 2.

**Method:** complete → pinpoint a breakdown → repair it → transfer to a new context → review. Aim for **local sentence → global article model** and **recognition → retrieval**.

## Listening · 6 min

**Task first (3 min):** Listen to the first ~3 minutes without the transcript. Write a 4-part gist from memory: **access problem → offline library → student benefit → remaining barrier**. Do not stop for names or statistics.

**Pinpoint and repair (2 min):** Locate the first place where your interpretation breaks: *whether or not*, *with or without*, a contrast involving **electric power**, or a shift from one student's experience to a general claim. Look up only the relevant transcript sentence, replay ~20 seconds, and say what the sentence contributes to the four-part gist.

**Retrieval (1 min):** Close the transcript. Explain in two English sentences why **offline** does not mean **free from all infrastructure needs**. The story reports benefits and power interruptions, not a controlled measure of learning gains.

**Actual evidence:** First confusing phrase/relation: ___ · Repaired meaning: ___

## Reading · 9 min

**Read the UNESCO report** with attention to the opening, “Connecting schools to the internet,” “Supporting teachers,” and the final evaluation sentence.

**1. Map paragraph functions (4 min):** Label the text **SETTING / INTERVENTION / INFRASTRUCTURE / TEACHER SUPPORT / LIMITATION OR NEXT STEP**. Underline one phrase in each section that justifies its function.

**2. Rebuild from memory (2 min):**

```text
PROBLEM: unequal access to ______
       ↓
PILOT: three schools; online + ______ learning
       ↓
INFRASTRUCTURE: satellite connection + devices + (at one site) ______
       ↓
HUMAN SUPPORT: teacher ______ and platform use
       ↓
BOUNDARY: impact still being ______; scaling is a future plan
```

**3. Evidence and scope (2 min):** What does “more than 5,500 students” describe: people the pilot aims to serve, or proven learning improvement? Why is the solar-power detail causally relevant? Does “allows for both online and offline learning” mean the schools never need electricity?

**4. Compress (1 min):** Without reopening the article, say: “The pilot addresses ___ by combining ___ with ___, but ___ still needs evaluation.”

**Local → global repair:** Parse “This allows for both online and offline learning, meaning staff and students can continue classes even without internet.” Identify the referent of **This** (installed ICT infrastructure), then the result introduced by **meaning**, then its limit (not necessarily without power). Return to the full problem–response–evaluation structure.

## Active Expressions · 4 min

### [[words/bridge-the-gap|bridge the gap]]

**Retrieve from meaning:** “弥合资源差距” → ___ the ___ in access to learning resources.

**Use:** An offline library can help **bridge the gap** between well-connected and underserved schools. Do not claim it eliminates every barrier.

**Transfer:** In a campus course, what could bridge the gap between beginners and experienced coders?

### [[words/make-accessible-to|make ... accessible to ...]]

**Retrieve from meaning:** “让没有稳定网络的学生也能获取课程资料” → ___ course materials ___ students with unreliable internet.

**Use:** Local servers can **make educational resources accessible to** learners even when connectivity is weak.

**Transfer:** Describe how captions can make a lecture accessible to a different group of students.

### Review · [[words/rely-on|rely on]]

**Retrieve:** “学生依赖稳定供电。” → Students ___ a stable power supply.

**Pronunciation/chunking (30 sec):** “BRIDGE the GAP / MAKE resources acCESSible / reLY on POWER.” Stress the content words; replace *power* with *teacher feedback* in a new sentence.

## Speaking · 4 min

**Topic: Learning beyond the internet**

1. What can students do with a local digital library?
2. Why can a school still face problems after installing offline resources?
3. How do the Nigerian university case and the Côte d’Ivoire pilot differ in scope and infrastructure?
4. If your university had unreliable internet for a week, which two resources should be available offline first? Why?
5. What evidence would convince you that an offline library actually improves learning?

Speak for ~60 seconds, use **bridge the gap** and **rely on**, and include one limitation. If you stall, record the missing idea before trying again.

## Writing · 5 min

**80–120 words, original writing:** **Should universities invest in offline digital libraries even when most students already have internet access?** Take a position, explain one real-life scenario (e.g., outages, accessibility, or fieldwork), acknowledge one cost or limitation, and propose one way to measure success. Retrieve one new expression without copying a model paragraph.

### 8-minute Busy Version

1. **2 min listening:** first two minutes; name the problem and the proposed solution.
2. **2 min reading:** read UNESCO opening + “Connecting schools”; identify infrastructure and one constraint.
