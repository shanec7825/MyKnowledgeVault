# Focus Under Pressure

**Guiding question:** When a performance matters, how can people shift attention from worrying about the outcome to doing the next useful action?

**30 minutes:** Listening **6** · Reading **9** · Active Expressions **4** · Speaking **4** · Writing **5** · Retrieval/Reflection **2**.

**Method:** Finish a task → name the *first exact* breakdown → repair it → transfer the idea to a different situation → retrieve without cues. Today, distinguish **an anecdote, an expert recommendation, and experimental proof**.

## Listening · 6 min

**Material:** VOA's *Negative Self-Talk Can Hurt You*. Play approximately **0:00–3:30**, stopping after the explanation of test anxiety and its possible pattern. The full recording is longer; you do not need to finish it today.

**First pass (3:30):** Listen without transcript. Hold only three nodes in mind: **STUDENT WORKS HARD → TEST PRESSURE → POSSIBLE ATTENTION/CONFIDENCE PROBLEM**. Say in one sentence why effort and exam performance might differ.

**Locate the breakdown (1:30):** Find the *first* sentence where you lost the logical relation. Was it a **contrast** (good preparation *but* poor performance), a **cause** (pressure), or a **reported opinion** (what the professor believes)? Open the transcript only around that sentence and replay it.

**Retrieval (1 min):** Close the transcript. Say: “The report describes ______. One explanation offered is ______. However, this does not prove ______.” Separate the reported claim from established evidence.

## Reading · 9 min

**Material:** Stanford Report, *How elite athletes handle performance pressure*. Read only the introduction and the answers to: **“What are some strategies …?”**, **“What are some examples of focus cues …?”**, **“Can any of these techniques apply to regular people?”**, and **“What are some common misconceptions about stress?”**.

**Pass 1 (4 min):** Assign each selected part a *function*, not a translation: **CONTEXT → PROBLEM → PRACTICAL STRATEGY → EXAMPLE → TRANSFER → QUALIFICATION**.

**Global model (2 min):** Close the article and reconstruct this *interpretive* chain in your own words:

    HIGH-STAKES SITUATION
          ↓
    ATTENTION DRIFTS TO OUTCOMES / WORRIES
          ↓
    SHORT, SPECIFIC FOCUS CUE
          ↓
    ATTENTION RETURNS TO THE NEXT ACTION
          ↓
    POSSIBLE BENEFIT: MORE TASK-FOCUSED PERFORMANCE
          ↘
           LIMIT: DIFFERENT PEOPLE AND SITUATIONS NEED DIFFERENT SUPPORT

**Evidence check (2 min):**
- What is a **focus cue**? Find one verbal and one non-verbal example in the interview.
- What does the expert **recommend**, and what does this interview **not experimentally establish**?
- How is *instructional self-talk* (directing attention to an action) different from *positive self-talk* (encouragement)?
- Does the article say all stress is harmful? Locate the qualification.

**One-sentence compression (1 min):** “Rather than ______, athletes may use ______ to ______, although ______.” Do not look back until after speaking.

**Visual retrieval:** In the HTML, inspect the attention-flow diagram for 20 seconds, hide the labels, then rebuild the chain aloud. Test whether you can select an **instructional cue** rather than an outcome-focused thought.

## Active Expressions · 4 min

1. **[[words/get-caught-up-in|get caught up in]]** — become so involved in a thought or activity that you lose sight of something else. **Chinese → English:** “不要陷入对结果的担忧。” → Don't ______ worrying about the result. **Transfer:** During debugging, I sometimes get caught up in tiny details.
2. **[[words/refocus-attention-on|refocus attention on]]** — deliberately bring attention back to a chosen task. **Chinese → English:** “把注意力重新集中到下一步。” → ______ the next step. **Transfer:** When reading a dense paper, refocus attention on the author's main claim.
3. **Review [[drafts/words/rather-than|rather than]]** — contrast a preferred action with an alternative. **Retrieval:** “与其盯着比分，不如专注下一次传球。” → ______ watching the score, focus on the next pass.

**Pronunciation and syntax (30 sec within 4 min):** Say in meaning groups: “Rather than GET caught UP in the SCORE / REfocus your atTENtion / on the NEXT MOVE.” Then replace *score* and *move* with an exam or programming example.

## Speaking · 4 min

**Topic: How do athletes and students handle pressure differently?**

1. What makes a basketball free throw or a university exam feel high-stakes?
2. What is the difference between thinking about the final score and focusing on the next action?
3. Give one **verbal** and one **visual/physical** focus cue from the Stanford interview.
4. How would you adapt a focus cue for reading a difficult English paragraph or debugging a program?
5. What evidence would you need before claiming a focus routine improves exam scores?

Speak from memory. Use **get caught up in**, **refocus attention on**, or **rather than**, and add a limitation with *although*.
