# Repair or Replace

![Topic illustration](assets/art/2026-10-14-Repair-or-Replace.svg)

**Question:** How should we decide whether to keep, repair, or replace an old electronic device?

**30 min:** Listening 6 · Reading 9 · Active Expressions 4 · Speaking 4 · Writing 5 · Retrieval/Reflection 2.

**Method:** complete → locate the first precise breakdown → repair comprehension → transfer → retrieve.

## Listening · 6 min

Listen to approximately the first 3:30 of the VOA audio without text. Identify **reason to keep a phone → what limits its usable life → response**. Spend 1:30 finding and replaying the first lost logical connection, using the transcript only at that point. Spend 1 minute summarizing the relationship without looking. Distinguish daily battery runtime from a device's total lifespan; separate advice from experimental evidence.

## Reading · 9 min

Read the EU article and the opening of the FTC consumer alert. In 4 minutes, label paragraph functions: **problem → barrier → policy response → expected benefit → limitation**. Close both pages for 2 minutes and rebuild the chain from memory. In 2 minutes answer: What makes a product difficult to repair? Which EU products are covered? Why does “free or reasonably priced” not mean “always free”? Does the US article describe EU law? Finally, compress in 1 minute: “Rather than ___, people may ___ when ___, although ___.” The EU announcement is about rules in its jurisdiction, not proof of savings at a particular university.

## Active Expressions · 4 min

1. [[words/extend-the-lifespan-of|extend the lifespan of]] — keep something usable longer. Retrieve from Chinese: “延长设备的使用寿命” → ___ a device. Transfer to older computers.
2. [[words/weigh-against|weigh X against Y]] — compare competing considerations. Retrieve: “权衡成本和环境影响” → ___ costs ___ environmental impact. Transfer to model accuracy versus computing cost.
3. Review [[drafts/words/rather-than|rather than]] — “与其马上换新，不如先比较选择” → ___ immediately replacing it, compare the options.

**Pronunciation:** “WEIGH the COST / aGAINST the BENefit.” Repeat in sense groups, then substitute a new pair of trade-offs.

## Speaking · 4 min

**Topic:** What makes a product worth keeping?
1. Why might people replace a device that still works?
2. How is saving power today different from extending a device's lifespan?
3. What obstacles do the two articles identify?
4. How would you weigh costs against benefits in a campus support service?
5. Under what conditions would replacement be more sensible?

Use one new expression and one qualification such as *although*.

## Writing · 5 min

**80–120 words:** **Should universities offer an affordable device-maintenance and repair service before encouraging students to buy new electronics?** Give a position, a campus example, a benefit, a limitation, and evidence that could change your mind. Use one new expression from memory.
