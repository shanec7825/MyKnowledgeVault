# Can perceptual learning be used to treat amblyopia beyond the critical period of visual development?

URL: https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3428831/
Captured: 2026-08-05 (autoresearch)
Verbatim status: 见各段标注；未标注段为逐字捕获。

> full text (PMC, verbatim)

> treatment is rarely offered to children beyond nine years of age, as it was assumed that the visual system lacked the necessary neural plasticity (the ability of neural systems to undergo change) to improve function at this age.

> We now know that there is not a single critical period for the developing visual system, but that the first 9 years of childhood represents the envelope of a number of overlapping periods, each of which have different start and end points.

> Those receiving perceptual learning improved by 0.25 logMAR on average. This would have required 385 hours of occlusion, yet the improvement was achieved in around 1/13th of this time.

> a large scale randomised trial carried out by the Paediatric Eye Disease Investigator Group (PEDIG)... showed that refractive correction alone can improve vision in 25% (50/203 subjects) of 7 to 12 year olds. Furthermore, a combination of refractive correction, occlusion therapy, atropine penalisation and near work improved vision in 53% (106/201 subjects) of those in the same age group.
