#### 16:54 · [Change Your Brain: Neuroscientist Dr. Andrew Huber…](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=SwQhKFMxmDY&t=4082s)

今天看到了这个访谈的24分钟，说实话，纯英文加上中文翻译字幕，一路看下来还是有点累的。
更多的是像看一个故事，不懂的英文在看了字幕之后明白了一些，但是英语读音还是没有过多关注。
我发现但我去通过单纯的音频理解意思的时候，我的大脑中的感受和读时是不一样的，声音似乎成了我理解意思的必要要素，单独看文字会感到脑子空空的，但是如果带着意思去听，声音中的语调语气似乎能够还原人与人之间对话的那种感觉，这种现象是为什么呢。
所以以后可以多试试听音频，音频中带有的信息其实并不比文字少，或许这是一个提高信息学习获取的方式。至于有没有副作用，比如降低文字的理解能力(虽然现在文字理解能力已经很弱了）。

> 
> **Andrew Huberman:** I did. My dad was a physicist, so scientists and big ideas were part of family life. I was fascinated by animals and how they behaved. But when I was about thirteen, my parents split up, and my dad moved overseas. My mom was struggling, and I started drifting away from school.
> 
> **Rich:** And you found a different kind of community?
> 
> **Andrew:** Yeah. I got into skateboarding and punk rock. I’d take the bus to San Francisco and spend time with a group of kids who were mostly unsupervised. It was exciting, and there were wonderful people there, but there was also a lot of dysfunction—drinking, drugs, violence, and people getting into serious trouble. I wasn’t a great skateboarder, but I loved the camaraderie.
> 
> **Rich:** School wasn’t going well either.
> 
> **Andrew:** Not at all. I barely attended high school, and eventually I was sent to a locked residential program for troubled kids. I was scared, and I realized I needed to change direction. A therapist helped me enormously. He saw that I had drive and curiosity, even though I had no real structure in my life. He encouraged me to build routines—to exercise, meditate, and take school seriously.
> 
> **Rich:** What finally made you commit to that path?
> 
> **Andrew:** After high school, I went to college, but at first I was still drifting—skipping classes and getting into fights. Then, after a particularly bad incident, I looked at my life and thought, *This can’t keep going.* I decided to stop partying, focus on fitness, and return to school. It began as a choice made partly out of fear, but it gave me a structure I could follow.
> 
> **Rich:** And then you found neuroscience?
> 
> **Andrew:** Yes. One professor taught me about the brain, mental illness, and how the body and brain interact. It connected with things I’d seen in my family and among my friends—depression, addiction, and schizophrenia. I became fascinated. A professor offered me a chance to work in a lab and told me that graduate school could pay me to do science. I decided to pursue a PhD and become a professor.
> 
> **Rich:** Looking back, do you feel grateful for those difficult years?
> 
> **Andrew:** I’m grateful for what they taught me, though I wouldn’t wish that upbringing on any child. I saw how much people need support and structure, and also how the brain can adapt and change. Those experiences shaped my interest in neuroscience—not just the biology of the brain, but fear, stress, trauma, and resilience. I learned that people can change, but that it often takes guidance, effort, and a supportive environment.
